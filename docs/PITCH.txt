BACKTRAIL — PITCH AND FEATURE PRIORITIES

THE PITCH TO COMMIT TO
Backtrail remembers the route, landmarks and things you meant to do on the way
back, entirely on your phone.

The hero is a connected workflow, not a claim to have the most features:
1. 'Remember this place and remind me to get my jacket on the way back.'
2. 'Take me back to the car.'
3. The app retrieves the real records, follows the recorded return path, helps
   compare the confusing junction with its saved view, and surfaces the reminder.

Gemma interprets intent, retrieves personal evidence and interprets a fresh
scene. Normal code controls route geometry, state, reminder triggers and timing.
The user's confirmation controls destination/action acceptance and progress.

WHY THIS IS WORTH TESTING
An ordinary map already provides route geometry. Existing apps also offer photo
waypoints, offline navigation and AR views. The proposed advantage is using the
meaning of a person's own trip records to retrieve the right evidence and help
complete a return task. This is a testable hypothesis, not a claim of originality
or a guarantee of winning.

RANKED FEATURE IDEAS

1. REMEMBER THIS FOR THE WAY BACK — core differentiator
   User: 'I left my jacket on this bench. Remind me when we come back.'
   Gemma: extracts a note/action bound to a current or identified saved checkpoint.
   Code: validates the checkpoint, stores the confirmed reminder, triggers it when
   the user returns to/explicitly confirms that checkpoint.
   Show: source photo + 'You told us your jacket was here.' Do not claim that the
   camera verifies it is still there unless a separate observation supports it.

2. FIND A PLACE BY WHAT YOU REMEMBER — core
   User: 'The bench before the wooden bridge.'
   Gemma: resolves description and ordering against the actual trip catalog.
   App: pans to the saved pin, opens its actual photo, offers Return here.
   Objection: if a simple label match performs equally well, the model adds less.

3. MATCH MY VIEW — core camera differentiation
   User: returning to a confusing fork.
   UI: saved return-facing photo over live preview, opacity slider or Then/Now
   divider. Alignment is manual until proven otherwise.
   Gemma: compares fresh/saved images and exposes visible evidence or uncertainty.
   Code/user: explicit confirmation advances the recorded route. Highest model
   feasibility risk; test first on the actual phone.

4. RETURN WITH A REMEMBERED TASK — simple extension
   User: 'Back to the car, and remind me about my jacket by the bridge.'
   Gemma: proposes a valid return destination and existing checkpoint reminder.
   Code: accepts only a reminder on the recorded return path. No arbitrary
   multi-stop route optimization or invented alternate trail.

5. IMPROVE THE MEMORY WHILE RECORDING — extension
   App: 'You changed direction. Save a return photo?'
   Rules: movement/heading quality, cooldown and dismissal; not a camera-free
   claim to have seen a fork.
   Gemma: after capture, suggest a more distinguishing fixed feature if the view
   is generic. Ordinary image checks can detect blur. Avoid constant prompts.

6. SHOW THE EVIDENCE — essential interaction quality
   Every answer links to an actual photo, user note, measured route fact or
   downloaded guide. The app preserves which facts came from which source.
   A model description never becomes a measured position or a confirmed fact.

7. THE MAP REMEMBERS THE WALK — core interface
   Pan/zoom a prepared offline region, show actual GPS trail and photo pins.
   Swipe the photo strip to move the highlighted map position through the trip.
   Gemma is not needed for drawing the map. The model's role is semantic access
   to these records. No claim that maps, photo pins or GPS retracing are new.

8. RETURN TO MY EARLIER OBSERVATION — extension
   User: 'Those planks were slippery; remind me when we come back.'
   Store and resurface the user's note at the recorded place. The message says
   'You marked this earlier' rather than claiming automatic hazard assessment.

9. CAMERA COMPASS — first rendering enhancement
   Approximate indicator toward the next waypoint along the recorded trail,
   reference landmark and distance over camera preview.
   Code: heading, true/magnetic north alignment, location quality and sensor axes.
   This is not ground-anchored AR or proof of a walkable straight-line path.

10. READ THE SIGN WE PASSED — stretch
    Retrieve a saved sign, show its image and bundled OCR text, and let Gemma
    explain the evidence. Preserve user corrections. Do not silently invent
    route distances, access permissions or unseen words.

11. A SMALL CONVERSATIONAL FIELD GUIDE — stretch after navigation
    Ask how to tie a useful knot, set up a simple tarp, or build/extinguish a
    permitted campfire. Retrieve a curated downloaded guide with source/date,
    explain terms, and show step cards plus original diagrams.
    A saved bulletin does not establish current fire restrictions. A general
    survival chatbot is not the reason to choose Backtrail.

12. ASK ABOUT RETURN TIME — stretch
    Interpret 'How far back is camp?' or 'Could I return before four?'
    Ordinary code estimates from recorded distance and explicit pace assumptions.
    Present uncertainty; do not let the LLM invent exact distances or a reliable
    wilderness arrival guarantee.

LATER, NOT AN EVENT PROMISE
Ground-anchored AR through long routes, relocalization after arbitrary wandering,
multiple trips/regions, voice input, long-hike battery validation and nature
field notes. No edible/not-poisonous, drinkable-water or safe-crossing verdicts.

45–60 SECOND PITCH, AFTER THE DEMONSTRATED BEHAVIOR EXISTS
'On an unfamiliar walk, you remember details: the fork with the blue sign,
where you stopped, or something to collect on the way back. Backtrail keeps those
details with your route, entirely on your phone. Tell it, remember this place
and remind me when we return. Later, ask for the place in your own words. It
retrieves your actual photo, shows the recorded route, and helps compare the
return view with what you saved. Let's turn off connectivity, let you choose a
destination, and change something in the scene. You will see the evidence it
uses, or where it cannot tell.'

THE DEMO
Record a short permitted outdoor route and genuine photos. Clearly identify any
pre-recorded route data. Disable Wi-Fi/cellular. Let the judge choose a saved
place descriptively. Show the map move to the real record. Compare a live scene
after the judge moves or obscures something. Show evidence/uncertainty. Confirm
the checkpoint and surface a previously user-confirmed return reminder.
No canned model results, hidden laptop inference or simulated GPS presented as
real. If the venue cannot support live GPS movement, label that limitation and
use the photo loop honestly.

HOW TO MAKE THE ENTRY STRONGER
Prove one useful situation, the model's connected roles, fully local operation,
and a judge-controlled variation. Make failure/uncertainty understandable. Keep
the demo repeatable and the sources inspectable. These are recommendations, not
an invented event rubric or winning probabilities.

GO / NO-GO
If actual visual comparison repeatedly confuses wrong places, stop polishing
that claim. If semantic recall/reminders add no value over photo pins, broadening
the chatbot will not repair the core product. Evaluate with real users/devices
before asserting time saved, reliability or long-term demand.
