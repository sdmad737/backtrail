What we would tell a user

Travis would rather say “not sure” than send you the wrong way.

The trade we chose

The lenient prompt found 3 of 3 true matches but called 9 of 25 different spots “the same.” The strict prompt made 0 wrong calls and found 1 of 3. A wrong “same spot” misleads a walker; “not sure” costs one more look. So Travis compares only against the spot your recorded route expects next, shows your own photo beside the answer, and leaves the decision to you.

Limits we know about

  • Small evidence: 8 real photos from one garden, 11 photos in the Mac evals, one Pixel.
  • 5 to 9 s per vision call. Fine for a junction; not continuous.
  • Once, a trip answer cited a wrong spot with confidence. That is why the cited photo is always shown.
  • No automatic GPU fallback yet if the NPU fails to start.
  • Night, snow and look-alike garage levels will produce “not sure.” That is honest, not a fix.

Next on the roadmap

  • Calibrate the live “lining up” meter (EmbeddingGemma at 0.3 s per frame) on real same-spot pairs.
  • Microphone capture for hands-free “Hey Travis” questions; the audio path already works.
  • A 200-pair eval across streets, trails and garages.
  • Step counter and compass into the right-way check.
  • Share a trip phone-to-phone, still with no server.
Say the trade in one breath, then stop. If a judge asks “so it misses matches?”, the answer is yes, by design, and here is the number. Never argue a limit away; name it and name the next step.
